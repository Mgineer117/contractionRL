# C2RL-PPO coordinates, AUC spikes, and code audit

This audit covers the local C2RL-PPO implementation, metric synthesis, actor/critic features, rewards, PPO update wrappers, normalization, checkpoints, MVE, and evaluation. It also reads the active [ksluamoy sweep](https://wandb.ai/UIUC-LIRA/isaac-car-baselines/sweeps/ksluamoy), the archived training source, and saved evaluation cases. No research training/evaluation experiment was launched; regression tests include short synthetic rollouts and optimizer steps. Static probes run on CPU on minjae-uiuc. Raw evidence is in `experiments/c2rl_diagnosis/20261018-c2rl-audit/`; the earlier fixed-bank analysis and figure are in `experiments/c2rl_diagnosis/20261018-sweep-spikes/`. The sweep uses seed 42, so these results do not establish behavior across training seeds.

## Changes made

As of 2026-10-20, C3M and C2RL-PPO CMGs receive physical state x only. For the car this is (p_x, p_y, theta, v), with periodic angle embeddings inside the network. The metric does not receive xref, reference parameters, controls or GRU context. Dynamics and dynamics Jacobians also retain physical coordinates; actor and critic tracking features retain relative positions.

C3M and CCM synthesis use the same independent uniform full-box samples of physical x, xref and applied-box uref. C3M trains both networks jointly on those triples. C2RL-PPO's CMG uses full-box samples, while PPO's actor and critic use on-policy rollouts. References enter controller training and tracking-energy errors, but do not enter the CMG.

The metric material derivative uses x_dot = f(x)+B(x)u. Reference motion still enters the tracking-error derivative. Real rewards, critic potentials and imagined rewards all evaluate the same physical-state metric M(x) at each endpoint.

C2RL-PPO defaults to direct CCM synthesis; explicitly selecting CV-STEM is also compatible with physical-state metric inputs. Relative-position metric checkpoints are rejected because their learned input coordinates differ. Running sweep source snapshots were not changed.

## Observed AUC behavior

Five selected run histories were refreshed sequentially and checked against their run IDs. At retrieval, the completed run [qf35d9xb](https://wandb.ai/UIUC-LIRA/isaac-car-baselines/runs/qf35d9xb) had 500,000 vector steps; [ilwgs5tv](https://wandb.ai/UIUC-LIRA/isaac-car-baselines/runs/ilwgs5tv) had 446,000, [lpg3teur](https://wandb.ai/UIUC-LIRA/isaac-car-baselines/runs/lpg3teur) 184,500, [152z0hk2](https://wandb.ai/UIUC-LIRA/isaac-car-baselines/runs/152z0hk2) 269,000, and [rrf8ekv0](https://wandb.ai/UIUC-LIRA/isaac-car-baselines/runs/rrf8ekv0) 115,500. These are W&B observations at the recorded retrieval times, not a scheduler completion audit. An initial concurrent sweep listing returned a duplicate ID; cross-run conclusions here use only the five sequentially verified histories.

The `Evaluation/Stability/auc_mean` training panel measures changing stochastic episodes, not a frozen policy evaluated repeatedly on a fixed bank. A 500-step episode can contain several PPO updates. The separate Bayesian objective, `Evaluation/Stability/validation_auc_mean`, is a final stochastic evaluation on 512 persisted cases with fixed reference, initial-state, gust and action-noise draws.

This pattern is consistent with a small bad tail dominating the average. The final saved 512-case qf35d9xb result makes that directly measurable: mean AUC 2.650, median 1.910, 95th percentile 4.131, maximum 122.752, and only 6/512 cases above 10. The worst five contribute 20.9% of total AUC. All cases completed, but three ended with error larger than their starting error. Completion is not tracking success.

The worst case starts at error norm 1.264 and ends at 25.94, with 36.2% of its actions clipped. The second starts at 1.061 and ends at 10.01, with 9.2% clipping. Mean clipping over the bank is only 0.58%. These are large physical tracking errors, not normalization artifacts: the minimum initial error in the bank is 0.292. Some high-AUC cases eventually recover, so high integral error and final divergence are different outcomes.

## Fundamental reasons and their evidence

**A feasible contracting input and a contracting learned policy are different requirements.** Metric training minimizes a+b'u+2*lambda*E over the bounded input box; PPO is not constrained to choose the minimizing or any other feasible input. A static probe of the saved final metric at the 512 actual initial states found the requested lambda=0.5 energy decay infeasible in 24 cases (4.69%). The applied deterministic mean violates it in 162 cases (31.64%), including 138 where a feasible input exists. Adding the first recorded gust leaves that fraction unchanged. The derivatives were checked against finite differences. These are instantaneous tests, not predicted failure rates; the two worst trajectories initially pass the mean-policy check, which also shows why a check at the initial state is insufficient.

**Full-box metric validation and near-reference tracking are different distributions.** The saved best metric reports 0.10% violations on its uniform-box validation sample, versus 4.69% at the actual evaluation initial states. With independent planar positions uniform on [-45,45], only about 0.0488% of pairs have both position errors within +/-1: approximately 49 of 100,000 triples before restricting heading or speed errors. A low average full-box loss does not establish the local servo behavior or a uniform certificate. Relative coordinates improve the representation; they do not remove this sampling gap.

**The optimization objective does not impose the reported performance measure.** Before the action-overflow penalty, the discounted sum of E_k-E_(k+1) is E_0-(1-gamma)*sum_(j=1)^(T-1) gamma^(j-1)*E_j-gamma^(T-1)*E_T. The sweep has gamma=0.9 and dt=0.03 s, an e-folding horizon of approximately 0.285 s, while AUC integrates normalized Euclidean error over 15 s. The reward is metric-weighted, the integral is Euclidean, and the RL criterion is expected discounted performance rather than worst-case stability. Steering affects position through heading, so recovering from some configurations requires more anticipation than this short horizon encourages.

**Exploration and physical disturbances require robustness beyond mean-policy stability.** Final action standard deviations remain 0.166 and 0.186 in qf35d9xb. Training AUC and final ranking both use sampled actions. Physical bounded gusts have hidden values and dwell times. Clipping changes the applied Gaussian action and limits recovery authority. The saved tail cases saturate much more than typical cases, but some bad cases never clip. Matched deterministic/stochastic and nominal/disturbed replays would be needed to determine each mechanism's causal contribution.

**Continued PPO updates protect sampled averages, not every earlier trajectory.** Minibatch KL early stopping and PPO clipping do not enforce contraction over the domain. A high-AUC batch may involve new initial conditions, references, gusts, action noise and several policy versions. Increasing value loss and raw advantage variance accompany several spikes, but those correlations do not establish which changed first. The metric is frozen, running state normalization is off in this campaign, and qf35d9xb has MVE off; those mechanisms are not necessary explanations for its spikes. The observed KL curves do not support a single oversized update as a sufficient explanation.

## Confirmed bugs fixed

1. **GAE crossed autoresets with timeout bootstrapping off.** `agents/utils/RL/agent_patches.py` used only `terminated` to stop the recursion when `time_limit_bootstrap=False`. A timeout followed by a new episode with reward 100 could leak that episode's reward into the previous one's advantages. The recursion now stops on either episode boundary; timeout bootstraps, when requested, are already added to the terminal transition reward. The active sweep has this option on, so this bug does not explain its qf35d9xb spikes.

2. **Initial angular error was unwrapped in AUC normalization.** `common/analytical_env.py::reset_idx` normalized later wrapped errors by an initial raw angular difference. For headings on opposite sides of +/-pi, that denominator was wrong and could make AUC look artificially small. It now uses the same wrapped error convention. Normal car reference starts avoid this seam; the saved worst cases are real failures independently of this fix.

3. **The optional analytic critic potential duplicated the CMG in critic checkpoint keys.** Attaching the metric as a registered critic submodule added `_pot_ccm_gen.*` keys, but a fresh critic had no such attached submodule before loading. The frozen metric is now an external reference; the outer agent saves it once. A fresh critic can load its weights, attach the restored CMG, and reproduce predictions. This option is off in the active sweep.

Current synthesis, rewards, diagnostics and MVE use physical-state metrics. Reference motion remains in tracking-error evolution. Plot labels formerly calling a configured-rate curve a theoretical bound now identify it as a reference envelope. Architectural spectral bounds alone do not establish that decay rate for PPO.

## Important unresolved issues and caveats

1. **The dense wrapped-angle energy has a real discontinuity.** `common/analytical_env.py::get_rewards` and `contraction/ccm_synthesis.py::ccm_residuals` use wrapped error with a dense quadratic metric. When angular error changes from +pi to -pi, a cross term 2*M_theta,j*e_theta*e_j changes sign. For fixed other errors, the limiting energy jump has magnitude |4*pi*sum_(j != theta) M_theta,j*e_j|. A static probe of the archived qf35d9xb metric used pairs only 0.0002 radians apart around this cut: median absolute energy jump 8.88, maximum 44.68, while metric entries differed by at most 0.000666. This can create an artificial reward jump and invalidates a global smooth-energy interpretation. It does not prove any particular logged failure crossed the cut. Relative position does not fix it. A continuous periodic/geodesic energy or an explicitly restricted tracking chart requires a separate mathematical change and validation; zeroing angular cross terms would also alter the contraction problem and was not done silently.

2. **Optional feature normalization changes the policy after PPO optimization.** `agents/utils/RL/env.py::install_feature_normalization` updates feature statistics after the PPO/MVE update returns. A static reproduction with the real factorized actor, no optimizer step, and unchanged weights changed its mean action by up to 2.41 and produced mean Gaussian KL 9.72 solely through the statistics update. Thus optimizer-only KL measurements do not cover the complete deployed-policy change when `use_state_norm=True`. This is off in the current campaign. Preserving the policy function when statistics change requires a separate implementation and validation.

3. **Value clipping and running value normalization can use different scales.** Installed skrl PPO normalizes old values with `train=True`, then updates the same scaler again while normalizing returns. With value clipping enabled, the saved old-value clipping center can be in a different scale from the current target/prediction. A static example stores an old value as 0 but represents that same value as -0.816 after the return-statistics update. The sweep's value clipping is zero, so that incorrect clipping center is inactive there. This is a dependency-path issue to fix if value clipping is enabled.

4. **Legacy exact-SOS checkpoints do not necessarily contain the deployed metric.** `C2RLAgent::_install_analytic_sos_metric` replaces `agent._ccm_gen`, while the existing `checkpoint_modules['cmg']` still refers to the original neural model. A static degree-zero SOS installation reproduced this object mismatch. Saving and then treating the original neural checkpoint as the exact metric is unsafe. This toy-system option is outside the car CCM sweep and outside the new relative-position path; its serialization needs a separate explicit metric-type/coefficients record.

5. **Online AUC excludes incomplete trajectories.** The metrics wrapper discards incomplete curves rather than fabricating tails, which is appropriate, but the surviving-case AUC is conditional on completion. Read it together with `Training/Learning/episode_completion`. A smaller survivor mean need not mean a better controller. The final Bayesian objective correctly rejects incomplete 512-case evaluation instead of rewarding omitted failures.

6. **Important diagnostics are filtered out of W&B.** `agents/utils/RL/logging.py::metric_name` drops AUC median/upper quantiles, post-update KL, explained variance, and early-end fractions. Some are computed internally. This limits what can be inferred from the public mean/stddev panels. Existing scalar-layout rules were preserved; diagnostics were saved locally for this audit instead of silently changing the logging format.

7. **The certificate and deployed process differ in several ways.** Metric residuals are sampled continuous-time nominal checks. The environment uses finite Euler steps, state clipping/wrapping, actuator clipping, bounded disturbances and sampled actions. A differential CCM condition is also not automatically a global finite-error quadratic-energy guarantee. MVE's independently sampled gust prior does not infer the hidden disturbance posterior from history. None of these approximations establishes uniform sampled-time stability.

8. **Optional and legacy paths have narrower coverage.** PPO's final incomplete rollout can be collected without another update; final step counts count environment interaction, not equal numbers of optimizer updates. C3M compact synthetic observations also do not reproduce the full joint distribution of generator context/history/preview seen on-policy, even though physical triples and relative features now match its requested sampling. Robot state boxes can include physically unrealizable combinations, such as projected-gravity vectors outside the unit sphere, where learned dynamics extrapolate. Full Isaac/PhysX simulation and all legacy switches were not exercised here.

## Validation and next discriminating checks

The full CPU suite passed 590 tests with three skipped. Focused tests additionally checked the final missing-normalizer-state rejection. Checks include relative-position input equality, moving-reference energy finite differences, actual/imaged reward equality, actor/critic feature consistency, checkpoint-frame rejection, normalizer round trips, angular initial-error normalization, analytic-potential loading, and GAE at timeout boundaries. Python compilation and whitespace checks passed. Tests that use simulator adapters do not establish full Isaac/PhysX runtime behavior. No new training run validates improved AUC.

The most useful next investigation is fixed-case replay of saved checkpoints with full states, applied/raw actions, energy residuals and relative-angle-cut crossings recorded. Pair deterministic and sampled actions, then nominal and disturbed plants. This distinguishes mean-controller failure, stochastic robustness, saturation and the energy discontinuity. Validate the metric on near-reference and policy-visited states in addition to the full box. Only then use matched training ablations for longer credit assignment, tail-sensitive objectives or an applied-control contraction constraint. Current histories and static probes identify real gaps, but do not determine a unique causal explanation for every AUC spike.
